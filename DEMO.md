# Three-minute demo

Run `./run.sh` and open http://127.0.0.1:4173. Start on **The detective**. Keep Forecast lab on **Original evaluation** for the main pitch.

## 0:00–0:45 — Let the judge guess

Use **Trust your snack memory?**, defaulting to Revels. Invite the judge to guess missing grams and the effective price increase, then **Lock guess & print receipt**.

Revels goes from 205g at £2.75 to 185g at £3.30 in the reported Morrisons comparison. The sticker rises 20%; replacing the missing 20g at the later rate costs a hypothetical £0.36. The former 205g amount would cost £3.66, giving **33.0%** higher cost per gram. Show the solid remaining weight and ghost missing weight. The overlay represents weight, not wrapper dimensions.

The decomposition is exact before rounding: +20.0 percentage points from sticker price, then +13.0 pp from restoring the missing grams. £3.66 is a proportional constant-weight calculation, not a pack that can be bought.

## 0:45–1:15 — Open the evidence

Use **Read sources & limitations**. Dates and product-identity uncertainty stay visible. Then show one recipe comparison: Digestives’ 2008 purchase record versus a 2022-updated specification, or Penguin’s earlier versus later coating description. These declaration snapshots are separate from size-change dates. An unspecified vegetable oil later being identified as palm oil does not establish a new substitution.

## 1:15–1:50 — Make a concrete decision

Open **Price comparison**. Select a historical retailer/date/category and request 300g. The whole-pack planner reports integer packs, checkout spend, actual weight and extra grams. For the recorded Tesco chocolate snapshot, 319.5g costs £2.90; for Sainsbury’s, 372.6g costs £2.70. These are separate historical snapshots, not live offers.

The exact objective is minimum checkout spend while reaching at least the target weight. Equal costs prefer less excess, then fewer packs. Ingredients and dietary suitability differ; loyalty eligibility, stock and quantity conditions are unknown. No extra discount tiers are invented.

## 1:50–3:00 — Test the prediction

Open **Forecast lab → Original evaluation → UK chocolate**. Distinguish source-backed product changes from the separately trained monthly category-index forecast. The selected histogram boosted trees score 1.57 pp average error over 33 later months, versus 1.98 for no change. History-only trees score 1.59, so the incremental commodity improvement is small.

Use the historical slider and **Reveal actual**. Show misses as well as successes. Then switch to crisps: Ridge scores 1.35 pp versus 1.26 for no change. The separate conservative demonstration output falls back to zero. **That rule was introduced after inspecting these test results; its future performance is untested.** Raw model forecasts, saved historical predictions and scores remain unchanged. Do not call it calibrated confidence or a validated regime detector.

## Supporting facts

- Ten documented size-change cases across nine products; nine have price pairs. The 2016 Maltesers prices remain unknown; the separate 2017 case is 103g→93g at a reported unchanged £1.50, retailer unspecified.
- Snickers uses reported RRP, not observed checkout prices. Recipe comparisons retain their own source dates and qualifications.
- Eleven independent 2024 listing histories and 56 same-day comparison observations remain separate from verified replacement evidence.
- The model uses monthly UK category outcomes and genuine weekly commodity/FX inputs. Thirty-four earlier validation targets select models; 33 later targets measure their errors, under stated publication-delay assumptions.
- Later model searches and RSI comparisons are retrospective exploratory work. The original default remains unchanged. The app does not yet predict when an individual brand will shrink.

## HRT emphasis: measure the value of extra data

Open **Forecast lab → Original evaluation → UK chocolate** and the **Does the extra data help?** panel. Ask whether weekly commodity inputs add useful information to monthly category-price forecasts. The saved experiment uses 34 earlier validation months for selection and 33 later months for evaluation.

The selected model’s MAE is 1.57 percentage points. Error is 20.3% lower than no change and 2.5% lower than a seasonal baseline. Compared with the same tree settings using price history alone (1.59 pp), weekly commodity inputs add a smaller 1.3% reduction. That last comparison measures the incremental feature contribution; the full 20.3% gain must not be attributed to commodities. These results do not establish causation or predict an individual product’s next shrink.
